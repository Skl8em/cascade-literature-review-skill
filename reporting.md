# Reporting

Templates for the three deliverables. Adapt the wording; keep the sections — each exists because its absence is what makes a review unauditable.

## Programme dossier

One per research programme, one to two pages.

```markdown
# Programme: <name>

**Hard core** — the propositions the programme does not give up.

**Seed and central works** — with DOIs.

**Axis 2 — dynamics: progressive | degenerating | indeterminate**
Evidence: stance distribution over the forward cascade (counts of extending /
rescuing / critical-empirical / affirming-applies). Then two or three anomalies
traced through the citing literature, with what the programme did with each.
Falsification hook: what would change this verdict.

**Axis 3 — contamination: contaminated | independent | indeterminate**
Diffusion channel, with evidence. Feedback path into the tested quantity, with
evidence. Approximate diffusion date. Direction (Barnesian / counterperformative).
Works published before diffusion, listed — these carry uncontaminated evidence.
Falsification hook.

**Inheritance** — papers assigned to this programme, and any paper whose
inherited verdict is overridden, with the reason.
```

## Review document

```markdown
# <Title>

## Question and scope
The theses under review, as propositions. Scope boundary. What the corpus
structurally cannot contain, and why.

## Method
Seeds, cascade directions and depth, sources and dates, tier counts, budget.
Point to the search log and the ledger.

## Corpus map
Programmes identified, their relative weight in the corpus, their axis 2 and 3
verdicts in a compact table.

## Synthesis
Organised by thesis, not by paper and not chronologically. For each thesis:
what is claimed, by which programme, on what evidence, at what claim strength
after axis-4 requalification, and with what reservations from axes 1-3.

State the warrant explicitly. "Reported as a robust regularity, but every
confirmation postdates the theory's diffusion into practice" is a usable finding.
"X has been shown" usually is not.

## What the corpus does not settle
Open disputes, indeterminate verdicts, and the questions no programme in the
corpus asks. Absences are findings only if they are written down.

## Adversarial pass
The strongest published objection to the review's own central conclusion, stated
at its strongest, with the response. If none was found, say so, and say whether
that is a fact about the debate or about the corpus boundary.

## Exclusion register
Every excluded work, with its three codes and its falsification hook.

## Verification status
Claims marked [V] were checked against the passage in the source. Claims marked
[C] were not. Both appear in the text.
```

## Exclusion register

Compact table, in the review itself rather than a separate file — an exclusion register nobody sees does no work.

## Formatting conventions

- Compact tables. Identifiers (DOI, OpenAlex ID) in the ledger, not scattered through the prose.
- Developed prose for reasoning; bullets only for enumerations that genuinely are lists.
- `[V]` / `[C]` inline on substantive claims.
- Quantitative figures, exact citations and dates unqualified only if verified; otherwise `[to verify]`.
- Quotations under fifteen words, attributed, and rare — paraphrase carries the argument.
- Date the review by project phase or by retrieval date, not by an implied permanence. The forward cascade has a shelf life.

## Handover note

Close with what a re-run would need: seeds, queries, retrieval dates, the tier thresholds used, and which programme verdicts are most likely to have moved. A review that cannot be re-run is a snapshot presented as a map.
